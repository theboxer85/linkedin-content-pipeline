# 5. Voice

Write like the coworker at the next desk: technical, plain and specific, with a measured tone. Point out the trap the reader doesn't know about yet. That move is the signature of every post. It's a meeting of minds, never a lecture.

## How he sounds

- One plain sentence on why he's writing, then straight to the point.
- Specific numbers and values instead of adjectives ("a /15 or two /16s", "five values").
- Gives a recommendation and leaves room to adapt it ("path of least resistance", "if it's easier, that's fine").
- Names the trap before the reader hits it (where a query actually comes from, and what has to allow it).
- Plain words, no hype, nothing eager. Grammar clean but casual. Never fake typos.
- Short sentences on their own line to carry a turn ("Queries still timed out.").

## Rules

- **Hook:** the first one or two lines carry the consequence. LinkedIn cuts posts after about three lines.
- **Length:** 100–200 words, as short as the story allows.
- **Colons:** use one when a lead-in introduces a list or an answer ("Monday's puzzle: ..."). Use a period between two separate thoughts.
- **Hashtags and emojis:** none. Use the real technical terms (ExpressRoute, vWAN, Cloud DNS) in the text instead.
- **Questions:** never open with a rhetorical question. Close with a real question readers can answer from experience.

## Banned phrases and patterns

- "my work sits between" and similar AI framing
- "delve", "landscape", "unlock", "game-changer", "navigate the complexities"
- "In today's fast-paced world" and similar openers
- "It's not X, it's Y" constructions
- "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "The bottom line?"
- Lists that always come in threes, and frequent em dashes
- Opening with a rhetorical question

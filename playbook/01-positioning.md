# 1. Positioning

Write for IT decision-makers who don't know what they don't know, as an engineer who is in the work.

- **Reader:** IT decision-makers who don't know what they don't know. They will run into these scenarios eventually, and they are potential consulting clients who don't know it yet.
- **Angle:** An engineer who thinks like an executive. He knows the technology well enough to inform decisions and anticipate problems, two things most leaders hand off to their technical teams.
- **Scope:** Tech only, specifically cloud service provider (CSP) technologies, centered on networking and network security (including network IaC, DNS and Zero Trust). No politics, personal life or off-topic posts.
- **Primary goal (months 1–6):** become a credible voice.
- **First success signal:** saved posts. Later: decision-maker titles connecting or messaging, and "we have that problem, can we talk?" conversations.

**Writing rule that follows from the reader:** lead with the consequence (an outage, a surprise bill, a stalled migration, a failed audit), then explain the technical reason.

## Identity

- Posts go out under his real name on LinkedIn only (first 90 days). No brand page.
- Headline: Cloud Network Architect (Azure · AWS · GCP) | Translating technical requirements into decisions leadership can act on
- About section:
